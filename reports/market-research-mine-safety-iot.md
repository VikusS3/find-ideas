## 3. The Problem & Who Has It

**Problem:** Underground mining remains one of the most dangerous occupations globally. Workers face risks from gas exposure, heat stress, fatigue, falls, equipment collisions, and ground instability. Current monitoring solutions are fragmented — gas monitors, fatigue cameras, and location tracking are separate systems with no unified dashboard. Real-time alerts are inconsistent, and data from incidents often arrives too late.

**Target user:** HSE (Health, Safety, Environment) managers at underground mining operations, particularly in Latin America (Chile, Peru, Colombia, Brazil) and Africa (DRC, South Africa, Ghana) where regulatory enforcement is intensifying.

**Geography:** Latin America and Africa — regions with growing mining activity and increasing ESG/safety compliance requirements from international investors and buyers.

**Assumptions:** Mining companies in target regions are under pressure from international investors and supply chain partners to meet safety standards; wearable tech costs are decreasing; 5G/LTE underground coverage is expanding.

## 4. Pain Point Evidence

- "Underground connectivity enables the transfer of data and communications... not all sites have this capability. Financial and geographical barriers are just a couple of challenges" — MineARC

## 9. Risks & Open Questions

- **Sensor durability underground:** Heat, dust, water, vibration; need ruggedized hardware (IP67+)
- **Underground connectivity:** Many mines lack reliable wireless infrastructure; edge processing needed
- **Worker privacy concerns:** Biometric monitoring may face resistance from unions and workers
- **Regulatory variability:** Safety requirements differ significantly by country
- **Hardware capital requirements:** Manufacturing sensors requires upfront investment vs. pure software
- **Long sales cycles:** Mining procurement and safety approval processes are slow
